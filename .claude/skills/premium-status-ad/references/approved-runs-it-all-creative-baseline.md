# Runs It All — Approved Creative Baseline

Status: APPROVED BASELINE
Use this as the default reference for future Runs It All paid ads, WhatsApp Status creatives, social posters, and campaign visuals unless the owner explicitly requests a different direction.

## Core Brand

Name: Runs It All
Slogan: Feel lighter.

Primary colors:
- Evergreen: #0F3D33
- Bone / warm cream: #F7F4EC
- Brass / warm gold: #C9A063

Typography:
- Inter family
- Premium, restrained, clean
- Strong hierarchy
- Spacious tracking for the brand name when appropriate

Logo:
- Use the approved Runs It All mark exactly as the canonical brand mark.
- The mark combines the rounded R/arrow construction with the brass circular element.
- Preferred use: top or centered with generous breathing room.
- Do not casually place the logo at the bottom of the creative.
- Do not reinterpret, redraw, glow, warp, recolor, or turn the logo into a decorative effect.
- Logo presence must be strategic: recognizable but never competing with the main message.
- Use the full brand lockup with “RUNS IT ALL” and “Feel lighter.” when the piece needs brand recognition.
- Use the symbol alone only when the format or composition clearly benefits from it.

## Approved Visual Taste

The approved direction is:

- hyper-realistic
- editorial
- cinematic
- premium
- calm
- trustworthy
- human
- contemporary
- high-end commercial photography
- believable real-world business environments

The desired perception at first glance:
“This is a serious premium company.”
“This looks real.”
“This feels trustworthy.”
“This looks expensive, but simple.”
“This company understands business owners.”

Avoid the obvious AI-generated look.

Do NOT use:
- exaggerated glassmorphism
- excessive glowing panels
- neon outlines
- glossy futuristic AI UI
- random floating cards everywhere
- fake sci-fi interfaces
- generic “AI company” visual language
- over-designed feature grids
- cluttered icon rows
- decorative gradients that exist only to look technological
- cartoonish humans
- plastic skin
- impossible hands
- fake-looking phones
- unrealistic screens
- stock-photo customer-support clichés

## Preferred Format

Default ad/poster ratio:
- 2:3 vertical

Reason:
- more compact and premium than an extremely tall 9:16 composition
- easier to control hierarchy
- still works strongly for mobile placements
- avoids unnecessary empty vertical length

9:16 can still be adapted when required by a platform, but the approved design language should first be solved in a tighter 2:3 composition.

## Human Realism Standard

When people are present:
- they must look genuinely photographed
- natural skin texture
- believable hands and fingers
- natural posture
- authentic facial expression
- appropriate wardrobe for the niche
- believable business environment
- realistic depth of field
- editorial lighting, not synthetic glow
- no “AI model” perfection

The person should be doing something that tells the story:
- resting after work
- cutting hair
- serving customers
- working while the system handles messages
- being away from the phone
- visibly gaining calm or time back

Never use a random smiling person with no relationship to the message.

## Phone / Device Realism Standard

Any phone shown must feel like a real premium smartphone:
- believable hardware proportions
- realistic camera placement
- realistic screen glass/reflections
- credible status bar
- credible message layouts
- no fantasy device UI
- no fake hologram floating out of the screen

If showing WhatsApp-like behavior, keep it believable and minimal.

A single convincing notification or booking confirmation is stronger than a fake full dashboard.

## Approved Composition

Default hierarchy:
1. Strategic logo / brand lockup
2. One dominant headline
3. One hero human or device scene
4. One proof moment
5. One low-friction CTA
6. Minimal reassurance

Keep strong negative space.

Use large, simple typography.

Every element must earn its place.

If the viewer cannot understand the point in about one second, simplify.

## Approved Copy Style

Use the simplest possible English.

Prefer:
- “You closed. WhatsApp didn’t.”
- “Your phone can be off. Your business doesn’t have to be.”
- “Stop losing bookings to late replies.”
- “Smart businesses don’t do it all manually.”
- “Try it free.”
- “Free credits included.”

Avoid:
- jargon
- technical explanations
- long feature descriptions
- abstract SaaS language
- “omnichannel”
- “conversational AI”
- “automation infrastructure”
- “digital transformation”

The viewer should understand the message instantly.

## Persuasion Layer

Use one or two strong triggers per creative:
- loss aversion
- contrast
- identity
- ease
- reason-giving
- reciprocity
- specificity
- social proof when real
- risk reversal

Do not stack too many persuasion devices.

The goal is to make the old manual way feel unnecessary, not to insult the customer.

Preferred identity framing:
- “Smart businesses don’t do it all manually.”
- “Your business needs a system. Not more of your time.”

## Offer Treatment

Primary:
Try it free →

Support:
Free credits included

Optional when verified and relevant:
- No card needed
- Keep your number

Do not overload the bottom with badges.

## Current Approved Creative Mood

The strongest recent executions use:
- deep evergreen salon/business interiors
- warm brass/gold architectural details
- real editorial photography
- cream headline typography
- realistic phones
- one booking confirmation
- one simple CTA
- calm owner body language
- brand logo at top/center
- almost no artificial glow

These are the baseline to improve from.

Future work should evolve this style by improving:
- realism
- composition
- hierarchy
- message clarity
- strategic logo placement
- first-look perception
- photography quality
- offer clarity

Do NOT reset the visual direction unless explicitly instructed.
