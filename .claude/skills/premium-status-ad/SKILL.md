# Runs It All — Premium Status Ad Creative

This skill is for **9:16 attention-first performance creative**. It combines direct-response psychology with premium art direction. The goal is not to make a “nice poster.” The goal is to make a person stop, understand, trust, and act within seconds.

Read these project skills first when present:
1. `brand-foundation`
2. `logo-sad` / approved logo assets
3. `runs-it-all-logo`
4. the current brand source of truth

## Brand Truth

Runs It All is a business operating assistant, not merely a WhatsApp chatbot. WhatsApp is the current wedge; the product can expand across customer conversations, follow-ups, booking, payments, reminders, reviews, feedback, broadcasts, channels, voice calls, analytics, and business-gap detection.

The customer transformation:
**The owner stops being the system. The business keeps moving.**

Core emotional outcome:
**control + calm + leverage + continuity**

Brand promise:
**Runs It All**
**Feel lighter.**

## The Performance Principle

A Status ad has almost no time.

Design for four moments:

### 0–1 second — STOP
The viewer must notice one thing before they swipe.

Use only ONE interrupt:
- a painful truth
- a visually unexpected business moment
- a large number
- a customer message
- a short contradiction
- a strong identity statement

Examples:
- **YOU CLOSED. WHATSAPP DIDN’T.**
- **Still answering every customer yourself?**
- **Your leads are already messaging you.**
- **You probably don’t need more leads.**
- **11 messages. You’re supposed to be resting.**

The hook must be understandable at a glance with no explanation.

### 1–3 seconds — RECOGNITION
Make the person say: **“That’s me.”**

Use one visual proof:
- 2–3 realistic incoming messages
- one missed follow-up
- one booking confirmation
- a phone lighting up while the owner is busy/resting
- one before/after contrast

Do NOT dump features here.

### 3–6 seconds — DESIRE
Show the changed reality.

Examples:
- customer gets replied to instantly
- booking completes automatically
- reminder goes out
- owner is no longer touching the phone
- lead gets followed up while the owner is working

Use one line:
**Now imagine this happening without you.**

### 6–9 seconds — ACTION
Reveal the offer with low friction.

Default:
**Try it free.**
**Free credits included.**
Optional reassurance when true:
**No card. Keep your number.**

CTA:
**Start free →**
or
**Try Runs It All →**

## Premium Art Direction Rules

Premium means **controlled**, not decorated.

### Hierarchy
Every creative must have:
1. one hero
2. one supporting proof
3. one action

If five elements compete, the creative fails.

### Copy Density
Aim for:
- Hook: 3–8 words
- Supporting line: 5–14 words
- Proof: visual, not paragraph
- CTA: 2–5 words

Do not explain the whole platform on one Status frame.

### Composition
- generous negative space
- one focal zone
- high contrast
- decisive type scale
- no equal-weight grid of features
- no six-icon “software brochure” layout
- avoid badge soup
- keep logo small but unmistakable unless it is a pure brand frame

### Typography
- large hook
- short line lengths
- use weight contrast before adding extra colors
- body text must remain readable on a phone at arm’s length
- do not use tiny legal-style copy to rescue an overloaded concept

### Color
Use evergreen as the dominant field when it improves recognition.
Use bone for warmth and calm.
Use brass sparingly as an accent, not a decorative flood.
Do not rely on gradients to create “premium.”

### Photography / Visuals
When using people:
- real business-owner context beats stock “customer support headset” imagery
- phone behavior should feel natural
- lighting should feel editorial and calm, not cheesy corporate
- show the tension or relief, not a random smiling model

When using UI:
- keep message snippets believable
- use only enough interface to prove the outcome
- do not fake an entire dashboard when one booking confirmation says more

## Direct-Response Copy Logic

Use this order:
**Pain → Cost → Desired state → Mechanism → Offer**

But most Status ads should show only 2–3 of those stages because minimalism is part of the conversion strategy.

### Strong pain territories
- late replies
- missed leads
- forgotten follow-ups
- phone dependence
- after-hours customer messages
- manual booking chaos
- no-shows
- missed reviews
- constant context switching
- owner becoming the bottleneck

### Strong outcome territories
- customers answered instantly
- bookings happen while owner works
- follow-ups happen automatically
- reviews requested automatically
- fewer customers fall through the cracks
- business stays responsive after hours
- owner gets time and mental space back

## Premium Trust Signals

Trust comes from restraint + specificity.

Prefer:
- one real-looking booking conversation
- one measurable claim only if verified
- clear free-trial language
- real product UI when available
- consistent brand identity
- no exaggerated “10X” language unless supported

Avoid:
- “revolutionary”
- “game-changing”
- “best AI ever”
- fabricated numbers
- crowded testimonial walls
- fake urgency

## Static Status Formats

### Format A — Pain Poster
Hero hook + one visual + CTA.

### Format B — Conversation Proof
One customer message → one automated reply → booking confirmation.

### Format C — Contrast
Left/top: owner doing it manually.
Right/bottom: system handles it.
Keep total copy extremely short.

### Format D — Statement
One powerful line on an iconic brand visual.
Example:
**Your business needs a system. Not more of your time.**

### Format E — Offer
For warm audiences:
**Try Runs It All free.**
Free credits.
One CTA.

## Motion Status / Reel Rules

Motion is premium when it is sparse and purposeful.

Use:
- notification arrival
- message typing → instant response
- booking confirmation
- subtle screen transition
- logo/slogan reveal

Avoid:
- everything flying in
- constant bouncing
- generic fade-up on every element
- fast cheap transitions
- excessive kinetic type

Suggested 9–12 second structure:
- 0–1.5s hook
- 1.5–4s pain proof
- 4–7s automated outcome
- 7–10s brand + free trial + CTA

Motion character:
**controlled, smooth, quiet, deliberate.**

## A/B Testing Discipline

Test ONE major variable at a time:
- hook
- hero visual
- proof style
- CTA
- offer framing

Do not call a color swap a meaningful creative test.

Recommended first test family:
A. **YOU CLOSED. WHATSAPP DIDN’T.**
B. **Still answering every customer yourself?**
C. **You probably don’t need more leads.**
D. **Your business needs a system. Not more of your time.**

Keep the rest of the ad stable enough to learn which hook actually wins.


## Persuasion Science Layer

Use ideas from *Yes!: 50 Scientifically Proven Ways to Be Persuasive* and Cialdini-style influence principles as a **filter**, not as a gimmick pile. The book's core lesson is that small, evidence-based changes in wording, framing, sequencing, and context can materially change response. Use no more than 1–2 primary persuasion triggers in one Status creative.

### Preferred triggers for Runs It All

- **Loss aversion:** make the cost of inaction visible. Example: “Every late reply can become a lost booking.”
- **Social proof:** when real proof exists, show what similar businesses are doing. Never invent customer counts, testimonials, or adoption claims.
- **Similarity:** speak in the exact world of the target niche. A salon owner should see salon messages, not generic SaaS language.
- **Commitment / consistency:** ask for the smallest natural next step. “Try it free” beats asking cold traffic to “Transform your business.”
- **Reciprocity:** free trial and free credits should feel like genuine value given first, not bait.
- **Reason-giving:** give one simple reason for the action. “Try it free — so you can see what your WhatsApp handles without you.”
- **Contrast:** show manual vs handled, late reply vs instant reply, owner-on-phone vs owner-free. One clean contrast is stronger than a feature dump.
- **Specificity:** concrete situations beat vague claims. “Can I book tomorrow?” is stronger than “automate customer engagement.”
- **Ease / processing fluency:** simple words, short sentences, familiar phrases, large readable type. If the viewer has to decode it, reject it.
- **Anchoring:** where appropriate, establish the larger hidden cost before presenting the low-friction trial. Do not use fake savings.
- **Scarcity:** use only when real and verifiable. Never fake countdowns or limited spots.
- **Authority / trust:** use credible product proof, real UI, clear brand identity, and verified claims instead of unsupported superlatives.

### Hormozi Simplicity Rule

Write so a busy owner understands it instantly.

Prefer:
- “Missed reply. Lost booking.”
- “You work. It follows up.”
- “Try it free.”
- “Your customers get answers. You get your time back.”

Reject:
- “Omnichannel conversational AI orchestration”
- “Customer lifecycle automation infrastructure”
- “AI-powered business transformation”

### Small-Change Test

Before shipping, ask:
1. Can one simpler word replace a technical word?
2. Can one sentence be removed?
3. Can the consequence be made more concrete?
4. Can the next step feel smaller and easier?
5. Can the same claim be made more believable by showing proof instead of saying it?
6. Is the persuasion trigger obvious enough to work but subtle enough to preserve trust?

### Ethical Guardrail

Persuasion must reduce friction and clarify value, not deceive. No fabricated proof, fake scarcity, hidden conditions, misleading guarantees, or fear claims that cannot be supported. Premium trust compounds; manipulation destroys it.
