# Runs It All — Master Creative Super-Prompt

Status: PRIMARY CREATIVE SOURCE OF TRUTH

Use this document as the default creative brief for all Runs It All ad concepts, social creatives, posters, campaign visuals, feed ads, Stories/Reels stills, LinkedIn creatives, and future visual experiments unless the owner explicitly overrides a point.

## Brand Core

Brand: Runs It All
Slogan: Feel lighter.

Core promise:
The customer-facing side of the business keeps moving without the owner having to do everything manually.

Emotional outcome:
control + calm + leverage + continuity + time back

Primary palette:
- Evergreen #0F3D33
- Bone #F7F4EC
- Brass #C9A063

Typography:
- Inter
- clean, premium, highly legible
- strong hierarchy
- restrained use of weight and tracking

Logo:
- use only the approved canonical Runs It All logo
- never redraw, reinterpret, glow, warp, distort, or approximate it
- place strategically, not habitually
- usually top or centered with breathing room when composition supports it
- logo should build memory without competing with the hook

## What the Approved References Mean

The approved reference posters are NOT templates to copy.

They are taste and quality references.

Preserve:
- realism
- premium restraint
- cinematic/editorial quality
- composition discipline
- attractive human casting
- strong first-glance clarity
- tasteful use of brand colors
- believable environments
- emotional pull
- simple, strong copy
- trustworthiness
- commercial polish

Do NOT mechanically repeat:
- a green salon
- the same room
- the same pose
- the same phone treatment
- the same layout
- the same message bubbles
- the same CTA placement
- the same type scale
- the same scene structure

Taste stays consistent. Concepts, environments, people, industries, and compositions should evolve.

## Creative Intelligence Standard

Do not only execute what is explicitly asked.

Act like a top-tier multidisciplinary advertising team and proactively consider:
- what will stop attention
- what will create curiosity
- what will improve retention
- what will trigger recognition
- what will make the viewer trust the brand
- what will make the old manual way feel irrational
- what will make the free trial feel easy to try
- what details create premium perception
- what visual cues create aspiration
- what is likely to perform on the target platform
- what could make the creative feel generic, fake, or AI-generated
- what is missing that the owner did not explicitly mention

Every creative should feel deliberately crafted, not generated.

## Human Casting Standard

Humans are the primary creative system.

Use attractive, aspirational adults, but never at the expense of realism.

Preferred cues:
- naturally attractive faces
- strong grooming and styling
- fit/well-proportioned appearance where appropriate
- expressive eyes and believable facial emotion
- real skin texture
- natural asymmetry
- believable age/detail
- authentic posture
- task-focused body language
- realistic hands and fingers
- real hair texture and flyaways
- appropriate wardrobe for the business
- premium but believable jewelry/accessories

Women:
- visually magnetic and aspirational
- attractive to both women and men without becoming sexualized or distracting from the business story
- confident, elegant, contemporary, natural

Men:
- polished, attractive, well-groomed, well-shaped
- strong editorial presence
- believable and professional

Reject:
- plastic skin
- impossible anatomy
- over-perfect symmetry
- generic AI beauty faces
- frozen smiles
- mannequin poses
- decorative hands
- unrealistic lashes, teeth, jewelry, watches, or hair
- characters that look like stock AI influencers

## Environment Standard

The setting must be specific and believable to the niche.

Examples:
- salon
- massage spa
- barbershop
- restaurant
- law office
- real estate office/showing
- gym or training studio
- event-planning environment
- boutique retail/service setting
- e-commerce operations space

Each environment should feel genuinely used and lived-in:
- realistic tools
- believable furniture
- credible materials
- natural clutter where appropriate
- proper lighting for the setting
- physically coherent reflections
- believable people in the background when needed
- no impossible architecture

Never force every creative into the evergreen salon aesthetic.

Brand color can appear through:
- wardrobe
- walls
- props
- typography
- accents
- lighting
- composition
without making every location look identical.

## Light and Life

Prefer brighter, more alive, more inviting premium photography than overly dark or heavy scenes.

Use:
- natural window light
- warm practical lights
- controlled contrast
- luminous skin tones
- realistic depth
- premium highlights
- clean shadows
- subtle warmth

Avoid:
- muddy dark greens
- synthetic neon glow
- excessive cinematic darkness
- overprocessed HDR
- artificial rim-light effects that scream AI

The image should feel expensive and alive.

## Phone, WhatsApp and Product Demonstration

Phone/product proof is OPTIONAL, not mandatory.

Use it when it strengthens the idea.

Possible modes:

### No phone
Use pure human/emotional storytelling when the scene and headline carry the message.

### Phone as subtle proof
A real phone may sit naturally on the counter, table, desk, or in the owner's hand.

### Message notification
Use one believable notification or reply state.

### Chat bubbles
Use chat bubbles when they demonstrate the mechanism clearly and add credibility.

### Booking/follow-up confirmation
Use one simple proof state such as:
- Replied ✓
- Follow-up sent ✓
- Booking confirmed ✓
- Reminder sent ✓

Rules:
- never let the generated image model invent important final UI text
- use realistic phone hardware
- important UI should be composited after the photograph
- preserve screen reflections, perspective, glass, glare, and occlusion
- avoid holograms and floating sci-fi panels
- no full fake dashboard unless a real product screenshot is intentionally used

Phone demonstration should feel like evidence, not decoration.

## Production Method — Mandatory

Never generate the finished ad in one pass.

### Pass 1 — Photographic plate
Create only the hero image:
- human(s)
- environment
- props
- real phone/device hardware if needed
- negative space for copy
- no logo
- no headline
- no CTA
- no fake chat bubbles
- no feature icons
- no marketing overlays

Audit the plate.

Reject if:
- human looks AI
- phone looks fake
- hands are wrong
- environment is incoherent
- pose feels staged
- materials/reflections are wrong
- lighting feels synthetic
- scene is unrelated to the selling idea

### Pass 2 — Controlled composition
Add:
- canonical Runs It All logo
- approved typography
- headline
- optional proof UI
- CTA
- minimal reassurance

Use real/controlled brand assets and compositing.

### Pass 3 — Final quality audit
Compare against approved references.

Reject anything that is:
- more synthetic
- more cluttered
- less premium
- less believable
- less strategically branded
- less clear
- more generic

## Message and Copy Standard

Use the simplest possible English.

One creative = one central idea.

Preferred structures:
- pain
- contrast
- identity
- relief
- missed opportunity
- time leverage
- customer experience
- proof
- risk reversal

Examples of strong language:
- You closed. WhatsApp didn’t.
- Your phone can be off. Your business doesn’t have to be.
- Stop losing bookings to late replies.
- Being busy shouldn’t cost you customers.
- You work. It follows up.
- Your next booking may already be waiting.
- Smart businesses don’t do it all manually.
- Try it free.
- Free credits included.

Avoid:
- jargon
- AI buzzwords
- technical terms
- long explanations
- feature dumps
- “game-changing”
- “revolutionary”
- unsupported guarantees
- fake scarcity
- exaggerated claims

## Persuasion Standard

Use only 1–2 primary persuasion principles per ad.

Preferred:
- loss aversion
- contrast
- identity
- specificity
- ease
- reciprocity
- reason-giving
- commitment/consistency
- social proof when real
- risk reversal

Goal:
Make the old manual behavior feel unnecessary or expensive, without insulting the viewer.

## Premium Composition Standard

Default static ad maximum:
- 1 headline
- 1 hero scene
- 1 proof moment
- 1 CTA
- 1 short reassurance line

Use negative space.

Avoid:
- feature grids by default
- icon rows by default
- multiple floating cards
- large explanatory blocks
- badge soup
- decorative gradients
- excessive UI

Premium = controlled.

## Format Defaults

- 4:5 = default cross-platform feed master for Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn
- 2:3 = premium poster / concept exploration
- 9:16 = Stories/Reels/Status only when needed

Adapt composition intentionally to each format; do not simply stretch or crop.

## Brainhead / Character Creative

Stylized characters are a secondary experimental system.

Use for:
- pattern interrupts
- educational concepts
- retargeting
- controlled A/B tests
- symbolic before/after storytelling

Default mix:
- 70–80% human-first premium creatives
- 20–30% character experiments

Do not make cartoon/brainhead characters the primary Runs It All brand face.

## Performance Standard

Do not judge success by:
- likes
- compliments
- prettiness alone

Judge by:
- attention/hold
- qualified clicks
- trial starts
- cost per trial
- conversion quality
- downstream customer quality

Creative testing:
- keep the offer and CTA stable when testing a hook
- change one meaningful variable at a time
- feed learnings back into the next creative round

## Anti-Bottleneck Rule

Do not endlessly redesign.

Per approved concept:
1. create up to 3 photographic plates
2. reject obvious misses
3. select 1
4. build 1 primary ad
5. create up to 2 meaningful variants
6. test

Do not reopen settled brand decisions unless:
- real performance data contradicts them
- the owner changes direction
- a platform constraint requires it
- a new product capability materially changes the proposition

## Final North Star

Every Runs It All creative should make the viewer feel:

“This is real.”
“This is premium.”
“This company understands my business.”
“This problem is costing me more than I realized.”
“This looks easy to try.”
“I should probably be using this.”

If the creative does not create at least three of those reactions, it is not finished.
