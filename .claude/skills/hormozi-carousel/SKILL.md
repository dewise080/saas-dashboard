# Runs It All — Hormozi-Style Carousel Skill

Use this skill for Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, and WhatsApp carousel-style educational posts.

## Goal

A carousel is not several posters.
It is one sales argument broken into simple steps.

Every slide must earn the next swipe.

The reader should finish with:
- a clear understanding of the problem
- a clear understanding of what Runs It All does
- a clear reason to care
- a low-friction next step

## Core Rules

- Use extremely simple English.
- Write so a 10-year-old can understand it.
- One idea per slide.
- Short sentences.
- No jargon.
- No feature dump on slide 1.
- Use pain and contrast before product detail.
- Show the cost of doing nothing.
- Make the solution feel obvious and easy.
- Do not insult the reader; challenge the old way of working.
- Premium means controlled, not crowded.

## Hormozi-Style Logic

Use this sequence:

1. **Hook the problem**
   - Make the pain concrete.
   - Example: "You may not need more leads. You may need to stop losing the ones already messaging you."

2. **Expose the hidden cost**
   - Late replies
   - Missed follow-ups
   - Forgotten bookings
   - No-shows
   - Lost reviews
   - Owner time

3. **Challenge the old way**
   - Identity framing.
   - Example: "If every WhatsApp reply still needs you, you don't have a system yet."

4. **Introduce the mechanism**
   - Explain Runs It All in plain English.
   - Avoid "AI automation" language.

5. **Show the core capabilities**
   - replies
   - books
   - remembers
   - follows up
   - sends reminders
   - requests reviews
   - gives insights
   - lets the owner take over

6. **Show the outcome**
   - fewer missed opportunities
   - more time
   - customers looked after
   - business keeps moving

7. **Reduce risk**
   - Try it free
   - Free credits included
   - No card / keep your number only when true and relevant

8. **CTA**
   - One clear action.

## Slide Count

Default: 5–7 slides.

Do not use 10+ slides unless the content truly needs it.

## Recommended 6-Slide Structure

### Slide 1 — Hook
3–10 words.
Big type.
No explanation.

### Slide 2 — Pain
Show 3–5 short problems.
Keep each line simple.

### Slide 3 — Identity / Contrast
Show why the manual way is the bottleneck.

### Slide 4 — What Runs It All Does
Group capabilities, don't dump features.

### Slide 5 — Outcome
Sell time, control, bookings, calm.

### Slide 6 — Offer / CTA
Runs It All
Feel lighter.
Try it free →
Free credits included.

## Premium Design Rules

- Use the approved Runs It All logo only.
- Never let image generation invent the logo.
- Evergreen #0F3D33
- Bone #F7F4EC
- Brass #C9A063
- Inter
- Strong negative space
- Large type
- Few decorative elements
- No glossy AI icons
- No glassmorphism by default
- No floating UI unless it proves the point
- No generic SaaS template look

## Carousel Visual Rhythm

Not every slide should look identical.

Use controlled variation:
- text-only slide
- simple statement slide
- one realistic human image
- one proof/chat slide
- one minimal capability slide
- one CTA slide

The taste stays consistent; the composition can change.

## Copy Fluency Filter

Before shipping, simplify:
- "customer engagement" → "customer replies"
- "automation" → "handles it for you"
- "lead recovery" → "brings unfinished bookings back"
- "CRM" → "keeps customer details and history"
- "analytics" → "shows what's happening in your business"

If a child cannot explain the slide after reading it once, rewrite it.

## Persuasion Layer

Use 1–2 triggers per carousel:
- loss aversion
- contrast
- identity
- specificity
- reciprocity
- ease
- social proof when real
- risk reversal

Do not stack every principle at once.

## Final Review

Reject the carousel if:
- slide 1 is weak
- too much text appears on any slide
- the product is explained before the pain is understood
- the reader must know technical terms
- multiple slides repeat the same thought
- the CTA appears without enough value buildup
- the design feels generic or AI-generated

## Default Runs It All Carousel North Star

The reader should think:
1. "That's exactly what happens to me."
2. "I didn't realize how much that costs me."
3. "Why am I still doing this manually?"
4. "Oh, Runs It All handles that."
5. "I should try this."
