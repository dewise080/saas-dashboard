# Logo SAD — Simple, Appropriate, Distinct

A logo is never judged in isolation. Judge it against the brand's emotional target, competitors, and every context in which the mark must live.

## 1. SIMPLE
A strong mark carries one clear idea and survives reduction.

Mandatory tests:
- black on white
- white on black
- 16px favicon
- 32px browser/UI icon
- square social avatar
- rounded-square app icon
- large hero usage
- single-color print
- grayscale
- embroidery / foil / etching mentality

Fail if:
- the logo needs a gradient or glow to become recognizable
- a different simplified drawing is required at small sizes
- the silhouette becomes ambiguous
- the wordmark and symbol feel like unrelated pieces

## 2. APPROPRIATE
The logo must produce the emotional target of the brand for the intended audience.

Ask:
- What does the mark make someone feel in two seconds?
- Does the weight, geometry, tension, rhythm, and typography match the brand promise?
- Is it serious enough for a business operating system while still feeling modern and accessible?
- Does it make sense for the audience rather than only matching founder taste?

Beautiful but emotionally wrong = fail.

## 3. DISTINCT
Distinctiveness is relative to the field.

Create a competitor shelf with at least 5 relevant logos. Compare silhouettes, architecture, color behavior, and common category clichés.

Run:
- cover-the-name test: hide the wordmark; is the symbol still ownable?
- disappearance test: does it vanish into the competitor lineup?
- famous-logo resemblance test
- category-default test
- color-swap test: if a competitor's colors were applied, would the logos become interchangeable?

A clean mark that resembles a major competitor still fails Distinct.

## Thematic Root
Every serious concept needs one thematic idea traceable to the product or brand story. The explanation must be specific.

Bad explanation: “Blue means trust and the curve means innovation.”
Good explanation: “Six independent paths compress into one continuous path, representing multiple customer operations becoming one coordinated system.”

## Exploration Families
Explore broadly across:
- custom wordmark
- letterform-derived symbol
- monogram
- abstract gesture
- geometric reduction
- pictorial metaphor only when it is not clichéd

Do not force one concept from every family. Keep only strong ideas.

## Selection Process
1. Generate many concepts.
2. Resolve them in black and white first.
3. Eliminate anything weak at 16px.
4. Eliminate anything visually close to competitors.
5. Score each surviving concept independently as PASS / WEAK / FAIL for Simple, Appropriate, Distinct.
6. One failed dimension sinks the concept.
7. Present the strongest 3 with evidence and a recommendation.

## Final Asset System
A selected logo must have:
- primary horizontal lockup
- standalone symbol
- inverse lockup
- black version
- white version
- app icon
- favicon
- clear-space rule
- minimum-size rule
- misuse rules

The lockup is one locked asset. Downstream design must not rebuild or respacing it.

## Runs It All bar
The logo must communicate one of the true brand ideas: orchestration, many-to-one, continuity, autonomous operation, command, or calm control. It must not be a generic SaaS “R” with a gradient unless the construction contains a genuinely ownable idea beyond the letter itself.
